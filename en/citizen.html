The Citizen

Speaking His Mind,
for Love of His Country

For almost four decades Phan Đình Diệu spoke to his country candidly and from the heart - as a scientist and citizen who believed that plain speaking was itself an act of patriotism.

Phan Đình Diệu never sought power, a party or a following. He was a mathematician who happened to be elected to the National Assembly, and who spoke openly and calmly about the fundamental problems of the nation and its society. His argument was consistent from the 1970s to the end of his life: that a nation cannot be governed by simplifying what is complex; that truth, information and open debate are the conditions of a country's strength; and that the nation itself must come before any doctrine.

Gathered below is the full record - every speech, proposal and article - with the original documents. A few passages are given in English alongside the Vietnamese; the documents themselves are in Vietnamese, except where an English version is marked.

In the National Assembly 1976–1981

As a young deputy, he brought the language of science into the chamber, warning that rigid, centralised management could not master a complex society, and that information and democracy were practical necessities, not slogans.

“The degree of democracy, in a sense, depends on how equally the right to own information is shared across society. Wherever that right is seized by a few, there is no democracy.”
“Mức độ dân chủ, theo một nghĩa nào đó, phụ thuộc vào mức độ bình đẳng về quyền sở hữu thông tin trong xã hội. Ở đâu có sự chiếm đoạt cá nhân cái quyền sở hữu ấy, thì ở đó không có dân chủ.”
- Address to the National Assembly, December 1980

The years of Đổi Mới 1986–1991

As Vietnam began its policy of renovation, he wrote widely in the press on democracy, honesty in public life, and national reconciliation - insisting that a country is strongest when it can draw on the talents of all its people, wherever they live.

“Democracy carries truth into power; despotism sets power to rule over truth.”
“Dân chủ mang chân lý vào quyền lực, còn chuyên chế đem quyền lực ngự trị lên chân lý.”
- Saigon Giải Phóng newspaper, August 1989

The 1991 Reform Proposal & the 1992 Constitution

In a formal proposal circulated in early 1991 - later issued in an English version - he set out, point by point, a path he believed could lift the country out of crisis. A year later, invited to comment on the draft constitution, he wrote on corruption, a free press, and the place of the Party within - not above - the nation.

“We should not compel our whole people to place any doctrine, or any individual, above their heads. The nation must come first.”
“Chúng ta cũng không nên bắt cả dân tộc ta phải đội lên đầu bất kỳ một chủ nghĩa nào, bất kỳ một cá nhân nào. Dân tộc phải trên hết.”
- Comments on the Draft Constitution, 1992

In the international press 1990s

In the 1990s his voice reached the international press, which reported on him with a mixture of curiosity and respect: a mathematician who spoke of democracy in the language of science, and who insisted he was a patriot, not a dissident.

“I am not a politician and I have no political ambition. But as a scientist and a patriotic citizen, I want to take part in the affairs of my country. Democracy is taking part in the affairs of one's country.”
“Tôi không phải là một chính khách và tôi không có tham vọng (chính trị). Song, với tư cách một nhà khoa học và một công dân yêu nước, tôi muốn tham gia việc nước. Dân chủ là tham gia việc nước.”
- Interview, Nordic Newsletter, April 1993
