The Poet

A Mass of Thought, a Mass of Feeling

Away from mathematics and politics, Phan Đình Diệu wrote poetry - most often to honour his teachers and companions, always in the classical Vietnamese forms, always turning back to the country.

His verse is quiet and formal, written in the seven-syllable, eight-line manner of the old scholars. It is where the private man shows through: his reverence for his teachers, his tenderness toward friends, and a love of country that runs beneath everything else he did. The two poems below are offered with plain prose translations - a bridge to the meaning, not a substitute for the music of the original.

In Memory of Professor Tạ Quang Bửu

“Viếng Anh” - for his teacher, the mathematician and educator Tạ Quang Bửu
Tiếng ViệtMột khối nghĩ suy, một khối tình Nước non là đó, nọ là mình Đã tròn một cuộc, bầu tâm huyết Chưa thỏa đôi bề, lẽ tử sinh Nghĩa nặng nhân tình còn quyến luyến Ánh ngời tài trí vẫn lung linh Nỗi đời chất chứa lòng ưu ái Một khối nghĩ suy, một khối tình
English (prose)A mass of thought, a mass of feeling. There stands the country; here stand I. One life made whole, a heart poured out - yet unfulfilled on either side, in the way of living and dying. Heavy with duty and human love, he lingers still; the bright light of his gift and mind still shimmers. The cares of a life, heaped up in a caring heart - a mass of thought, a mass of feeling.

In Honour of Professor Trần Đại Nghĩa

“Tặng Anh” - for the engineer who left Paris to build Vietnam's wartime arsenal
Tiếng ViệtNghĩa lớn gọi về với nước non Buồn vui đã trải cuộc vuông tròn Rèn tài văn võ thời phiêu bạt Gánh việc giang sơn thuở mất còn Tình nặng ấy chưng tình đất nước Nghiệp đời há kể nghiệp vàng son Gốc thông đứng thẳng dầu mưa nắng Để gió lành reo ngát nước non
English (prose)A great cause called him home to the country. Through sorrow and joy he lived a whole, rounded life. He honed his gifts in letters and in arms through his wandering years, and shouldered the nation's work in times of survival and loss. That heavy love was love of the homeland - what is a lifetime's career beside it? Like a pine standing straight through rain and sun, he lets a kind wind sing, fragrant, across the country.

These are working prose translations. Vietnamese classical verse carries a strict tonal music and seven-syllable line that no literal rendering can reproduce; the originals are given alongside so the reader can hear what the translation can only point toward.
