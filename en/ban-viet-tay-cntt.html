The Handwritten Draft · circa 1994

National Program on Information Technology - Master Plan to 2000

"Chương trình Quốc gia về Công nghệ thông tin - Kế hoạch tổng thể đến năm 2000" (bản thảo viết tay)
In the hand of Phan Đình Diệu
The author's original 45-page manuscript, from which the official plan (Decision 211/TTg, 1995) was drawn almost verbatim
This is Phan Đình Diệu's own handwritten draft of Vietnam's first National IT Program. Nearly every point of the official plan signed by Prime Minister Võ Văn Kiệt in 1995 was taken, almost word for word, from this manuscript - but the draft is richer, and several passages never made it into the official version. The most important is the argument for connecting Vietnam to the Internet, on pages 12-13. Vietnam would go online in 1997 - a relatively early step for a country still so cautious about opening to the outside world, owed to the foresight and patient persuasion of Phan Đình Diệu and like-minded colleagues, together with the progressive-minded leaders who backed them within the government, especially Prime Minister Võ Văn Kiệt. The draft also carries the author's own structure (Parts I-V), a footnote comparing IT workforces abroad (page 6), and a whole closing section on Berne-Convention copyright and software protection (page 45) - none of it in the signed plan.
Note on the transcription and translation. Each page below shows the original handwritten scan, a Vietnamese transcription, and an English translation. The manuscript is a working draft - written in the author's hand, with many crossings-out and insertions - so some readings are uncertain (marked with [?]) and heavily reworked passages are best-effort reconstructions. The transcription was produced with the help of Claude (an AI assistant), using the official typed text to disambiguate the handwriting, then reviewed. For anything that matters, please consult the scanned image alongside each page - and if you notice an error, please let us know.

Vietnam in 1993-1995: the starting point

To measure the draft's ambition, one has to picture the country it was written for. Vietnam was still in the early years of Đổi Mới, only beginning to open after decades of war and isolation. The United States trade embargo was not lifted until February 1994, and diplomatic normalisation with Washington and Vietnam's entry into ASEAN both came in July 1995 - the very months this master plan took effect. It was, quite literally, a document written at the threshold of the country's re-entry into the world.

On the ground, the technological base was almost nonexistent. Vietnam was among the poorest countries in the world, with income of only a few hundred dollars a head. Telephones were a luxury - far fewer than one line per hundred people - and the public Internet would not arrive until November 1997. Personal computers were scarce, expensive imports; there were no credit cards and no electronic payments; almost every transaction, public or private, was settled in cash. Formal computing expertise existed in only a handful of institutes and universities.

It is against this backdrop - no Internet, no cashless payment, no domestic software industry, barely a telephone network - that the manuscript calmly wrote of connecting to the global Internet, of electronic funds transfer, of networks becoming the marketplace, and of Vietnam becoming an information society. What reads today as common sense was, in 1993, an act of imagination: describing a country that did not yet exist, and charting the course to build it.

Ideas ahead of their time

Points from the manuscript that were carried into the plan signed by Prime Minister Võ Văn Kiệt - and that, seen from today, read less like a 1995 government document than a roadmap of the 21st-century digital economy.

1 An "information society" as a national goal

IT is framed not as office equipment but as a force to reshape social life and give Vietnam "a worthy position in the region as it enters the 21st century" - anticipating a global discourse that peaked only with the UN World Summit on the Information Society a decade later.

"CNTT sẽ góp phần chủ yếu đưa xã hội ta phát triển theo hướng hình thành một 'xã hội thông tin'."

2 Connecting Vietnam to the global Internet

A national data network for education and research, linked through a common gateway to the international Internet. Vietnam went online only in November 1997; the manuscript argues for it here, on pages 12-13 - a passage largely dropped from the signed version.

"…qua một cửa khẩu chung nối được với mạng Internet quốc tế."

3 Software first - and built for export

Priority to the software industry rather than hardware, with a software park and an export-oriented project, and even the direction "software-based hardware." It foresaw the outsourcing and product-software boom that would define Vietnamese tech from the 2000s.

"Cần ưu tiên cho việc phát triển công nghiệp phần mềm… hướng tới thị trường xuất khẩu."

4 Open systems and a Vietnamese character standard

"Open systems" as a guiding principle - to cut waste and avoid vendor lock-in - with a framework of standards covering operating services, interfaces, databases, networking, and a standard encoding for Vietnamese characters, years before the Unicode/TCVN debates.

"…'hệ thống mở'… các vấn đề đặc thù Việt Nam (như chuẩn mã chữ Việt…)."

5 National databases and e-government

Long before the term existed: national databases and the computerisation of state management, with an integrated database at the Prime Minister's Office linked to ministries and provinces for timely decision-making.

"Xây dựng cơ sở dữ liệu tổng hợp tại Văn phòng Chính phủ, nối với… các Bộ, ngành, địa phương."

6 Electronic payments and a cashless economy

Expanding non-cash payment among the public, electronic clearing and funds transfer, and a modern national payment system - a vision realised in Vietnam only in the 2020s.

"…thanh toán bù trừ điện tử và thanh toán chuyển tiền điện tử."

7 E-commerce - the network as marketplace

The commercial-information network is expected to become "the principal place where businesses carry out transactions" - a plain description of e-commerce in a country then almost without consumer connectivity.

"…mạng sẽ là nơi chủ yếu để các doanh nghiệp tiến hành các giao dịch thương mại."

8 "Smart" products - embedded computing

Embedding electronics and computing into industrial products to create "smart" machines and devices that replace purely mechanical equipment - an early articulation of embedded systems and today's Internet of Things.

"…tạo ra các thiết bị, máy móc 'thông minh'."

9 Digitising cultural heritage, including Hán-Nôm

Using IT to preserve national heritage - building databases of heritage materials including the Nôm script and Hán-Nôm texts as graphics, so they could be conserved and made usable for research. A forerunner of digital humanities.

"…xây dựng các kho dữ liệu về các di sản văn hoá, như chữ Nôm và các văn bản Hán-Nôm…"

10 People first - bilingual training and the diaspora

Human resources called "the most important measure": no fewer than 20,000 IT professionals by 2000, IT literacy in schools, teaching IT in both Vietnamese and English, and drawing on overseas-Vietnamese (Việt Kiều) experts - treating global integration as a core asset.

"…giảng dạy về CNTT cả bằng tiếng Việt và tiếng Anh… làm việc thuận lợi với nước ngoài."

In this master plan we understand the foundation-structure of Information Technology that we are building as comprising all the foundational elements that constitute the IT branch itself, in its capacity as an economic-technical branch of the country, and those forces are the basis for IT to be applied and to have a positive impact on the development and modernization of the other branches of the entire socio-economic system. From that viewpoint, building the IT foundation-structure ranges from building and bringing into play human-resource potential to developing the material-technical facilities of IT. This Part will present the content and the principal measures of the plan for building that foundation-structure, comprising the following sections:

2.1. Education and training in IT
2.2. Research and development in IT
2.3. Development of the IT industry
2.4. Building and developing data-communications networks
2.5. Standards and open systems

2.5. Standards and open systems.

a. The viewpoint on open systems.

Resolution 49/CP on the development of IT regarded "open systems" as one of the guiding viewpoints of IT development in our country, aiming to increase effectiveness and reduce waste, ensuring consistency among the parts of the whole IT-development process: from developing the IT infrastructure, IT education and training, building the IT industry, and developing the informatized systems in IT applications.

Developing IT according to the open-systems viewpoint requires defining and adhering to a system of standards and groups of standards - these standards being chosen to accord with the prevailing development trends of modern IT. For example, their content is the specifications of the interfaces between the parts and layers of an information-technology system, so that every IT product, though produced from different sources, from different manufacturers, will - if all conform to those standards - be able to be assembled and interconnected with one another. Besides, there are other kinds of standards, and defining those kinds of standards is one of the matters requiring attention.

In the time ahead, one of the important measures to ensure that the standards and open systems are built and implemented is the policies and legal regulations on IT.
