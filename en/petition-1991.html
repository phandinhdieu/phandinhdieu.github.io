Petition · 1991

Petition for an Urgent Program to Overcome the Crisis and to Create Sound Conditions for National Development

Phan Đình Diệu
1991
This is the full text of the English version of the 1991 Petition, transcribed from the original typescript. A few passages faded in the scan have been restored by cross-reference with the Vietnamese original. The scanned PDF is also available.

Preface

The nation remains in a state of crisis. Vacillating and irresolute reform measures in recent years have changed somewhat the face of society, yet are inadequate in pulling the nation out of crisis. It is impossible to fulfill any normal development plan in this state of total crisis. Therefore, it is necessary to analyze in detail the substance and characteristics of the crisis, to scientifically determine one objective goal that corresponds to the real needs of national development in this period, from which can be found resolute solutions to overcome the crisis and to create the sound conditions necessary for development along such a course. This program summarizes several petitions regarding these problems.

I. Our Country's Total Crisis at Present

I.1  The true condition of society

After decades of unending war, victory in 1975 and national reunification have created a rare opportunity for national unity and concord, for mobilizing and exercising all potential in society toward rebuilding and developing a prosperous nation integrated into a rapidly changing world. Regrettably, this opportunity was not fully exploited. After reunification, a socialist model was hurriedly imposed upon the entire country. The first effects are to place into opposition a large segment of the real potential that could contribute to the country's prosperity, and to eliminate the possibility for national reconciliation and concord. And, in the last decade or more, this imposition has bred a regime of centralized power that controls every facet of economic, political, ideological, cultural and social activities in the country.

This apparatus of centralized power - through the slow enhancement, on the one hand, with today's new ideas and with intellectual capabilities befitting life's demands, and through a gradual degeneration, on the other hand, from the evils of special privileges and powers, bureaucracy, corruption, … - has become impotent in organizing and managing the healthy development of the country. Managing through a power monopoly but in impotence has led, on the one hand, to the State constantly putting forward subjective and unrealistic plans of great harm and waste while, on the other hand, to the society spontaneously evolving along a chaotic path through every legal and illegal "destructive" scheme, with deceitful business practices becoming a widespread phenomenon.

Thus, this centralized but impotent administration has paralyzed a large portion of the nation's real potential and, at the same time, has spawned chaos and deceit in every facet of economic and social activity. An economy with chronic, at times galloping, inflation, with stagnant production, with the working people's standard-of-living in steep decline, with pitiful wages unable to guarantee a minimum livelihood, have led to the degeneration of the State apparatus and the entire society, to the decline in health and education, to the spread of social ills, to the pervasiveness of the abuse of power and corruption - these are the fundamental characteristics of the condition of total crisis endured by our nation in recent years.

I.2  The process of reform

After the Vietnamese Communist Party's VIth Congress, the task of renovation has proceeded with many complex changes. The contents of renovation were determined by the Congress along these main lines: to transform the economy from a centralized subsidizing bureaucratic regime into a commodity economy with many participants under State regulation, and to broaden democracy "under the Party's leadership." The renovation process has engendered colorful strokes in social life, especially in the first few years. Nevertheless, lacking a consistent scientific theoretical base, while stumbling before conservative reaction - especially, toward the events in Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union, of those forces clinging to their privileges - the renovation process has stalled in many instances. Economic renovation has been fragmentary, intermittently loosening and tightening, and is unable to create the confidence necessary for sound development, while the tendency for chaos has not been overcome. Politically, every democratic right and freedom - including freedom of thought, freedom of expression and of the press - has been harshly prohibited.

Renovation is an urgent demand. The reform process has clearly borne results that have made life less stifling and more bearable. But at the same time, the hesitant and fragmentary reform policies and measures are clearly inadequate to spawn and develop the positive factors, while the old negative factors have occasion to be supplemented with new ones. Honest bourgeois businessmen have not appeared in numbers, yet a "hooligan bourgeois" stratum has quickly emerged to collude with all elements who wield special privileges and powers in the State apparatus to further monopolize and destroy the country's economy. Halfheartedness has also prevented correct measures from being effectively implemented - for example, the anti-inflation measures in 1989 were necessary and did bring immediate results, yet this "situation" was not fully utilized for the realization of more resolute and fundamental measures, so that when the situation has passed, the threat exists for even worse inflation to return.

I.3  Urgent demands

Clearly, fragmentary renovation measures are not adequate to free the country from today's serious crisis. The task of renovation must be implemented more resolutely and consistently: resoluteness and consistency in determining one realistic objective for national development, and also resoluteness and consistency in implementing necessary, possibly severe, measures to overcome the crisis, to eliminate all obstacles, to create a sound environment for development toward that objective.

II. Objective

A developed market economy and a political regime with democracy, national unity and concord.

II.1  Viewpoint on the objective

The August 1945 Revolution opened a new era for the entire Vietnamese nation on the path toward fulfilling the profound dream of independence, freedom and happiness for the country. Three decades of arduous and valiant struggle have brought independence and unification to the nation. Afterwards, a socialist regime with an economy under centralized State management and with a political regime under the Party leadership was imposed upon the country. Decades of prolonged stagnation, recession and crisis are adequate proof that this choice is not suitable for achieving freedom, independence and happiness for the nation.

Our dream of freedom, independence and happiness for the nation is also the common dream of humanity for a prosperous and just society. Which social regime can best guarantee the fulfillment of such dreams? Socio-political theory of the XIXth century put forward two radically different models: a market economy with total and absolute freedom of competition, and its logical negation, ie, a non-commodity economy under a regime of central planning. And humanity has witnessed the shortcomings of both models in actual practice, with all their contradictions, interactions, and dynamics. In the XXth century, along with free competition have gradually been supplemented, at varying degrees and character, with centralized State regulation, while the non-commodity centrally managed economies, established in the "socialist" countries, have stagnated for years, collapsed under crisis, and now must revert with difficulty to the market economy.

Socio-political theory in our era, bolstered by modern scientific theory, has clearly demonstrated the shortcomings of the above radical models and, from this, is pointing toward a rational model with the potential of attaining the dream of prosperity and justice: a market economy under State macro-regulation as well as a political regime with democracy, national unity and concord. This is the general tendency of the era, the aim that many nations are striving for. We must also assert this tendency as the objective for our nation.

II.2  A developed market economy with State macro-regulation

After the Vietnamese Communist Party's VIth Congress in 1986, we have gradually shifted to a commodity economy with many participants. Nevertheless, many problems have not been satisfactorily resolved, mainly due to deadlocks between objective demands to escape from the crisis and subjective desires to perpetuate old viewpoints of socialism with centralized management, State ownership and a nationalized economy. Thus have arisen many self-contradictory notions, such as a planned commodity economy, a commodity economy following a socialist path, a market economy with State and collective enterprises as foundation, …

A market economy with an adequate respect for free enterprise and for equality between economic players, with the free exchange of goods whose prices are determined by supply-and-demand, … is a prevalent system for fostering the incentives of economic gain, for developing every capacity to enrich society. Ours is an economy that has emerged from backward agricultural production, without traversing through an advanced commodity economy. Therefore, once a market economy has been selected as the direction, then every favorable condition must be created and encouraged to take shape and develop, instead of being treated with indifference through discrimination and restrictions.

A developed market economy is a society with free exchange of goods among the multitude of economic players. Of course, each economic player must be an owner. In the final analysis, developing a market economy requires the recognition and respect for the right to private ownership, including of the means of production. And because a majority of the property is in the hands of the State, a shift to a market economy necessitates a process of privatization in conjunction with shrinkage of the State sector. Society must create conditions so that each citizen is able to be a private owner, instead of depriving and coalescing private ownership into a "people's ownership" always in danger of becoming the irresponsible private property of those few enjoying special powers. Society must create conditions for the exercise of every capability in production and enterprise, instead of finding ways to maintain the "leading" role for the State sector, which has often been proven to be inefficient and in the red. In today's period of transition, the integrated use of "privatization" must be figured as an important means for overcoming inflation and crisis. From the forms of private and State ownership will emerge diverse kinds of enterprises: private economy (including private factories), collectives, stock-ownership companies, State factories, … In agriculture, the right to land ownership by the peasantry (land-to-the-tiller) must be recognized so that the peasant can choose either the family or collective mode of production.

The function of the State in the economy is to create a favorable environment for the rapid and graceful development of the market economy, to create the infrastructure for systematic intermingling of the entire economy, and to regulate economic development at the macro level. There is need for an early perfection of the system of economic law, of a nature that will promote the development of a market economy inside the country and the expansion of external economic relations. The State regulates the economy by deciding on outline plans, and by realizing them principally through financial and monetary measures (taxation, interest rates for investment loans, …). The State economic sector will be comprised of branches that bear the infrastructure for the whole system, ie, energy, communications, and transport (mainly rail, air, north-south and overseas shipping), and possibly of highly profitable branches, such as liquor, beer, cigarettes, to provide income for the budget; other branches will be gradually transferred in whole or in part to the private or joint private-State sector through stock-ownership companies.

II.3  A political regime with democracy, national unity and concord

A market economy that fully respects freedom of enterprise and of commodity exchange by each member of society must necessarily be reinforced with a political regime that respects the democratic freedoms of each person. These are a person's and a citizen's fundamental rights, which humanity has struggled for many centuries to gain: the right to life and to the pursuit of happiness, the right to the inviolability of person, freedom of movement and residence, freedom of thought and creativity, freedom of expression and of press, the right to vote and to be elected into State organs of power, freedom of association, …

A person who fully enjoys these rights is genuinely a master, with the opportunity to develop all capacity and intellect, to enrich oneself, and to contribute to the nation's wealth. Naturally, the legal system in society must guarantee that the exercise of these rights by one person is not detrimental to the exercise of the same rights by another person.

A democratic political system that guarantees these fundamental rights of a citizen is in itself an acknowledgment of the pluralist nature. Pluralism is the authentic reflection of the objective reality of society into the body politic. Democracy is only the respect for the authenticity of that reflection. To deny the pluralist nature also means to deny democracy. This reality must be recognized: that there is no true democracy in a regime where monopoly of power and centralized leadership by a party have already been pre-determined. However, pluralism is not equivalent to a situation with many parties in opposition, in struggle to eliminate each other, even to the point of using force for this purpose. The democratic political system in our conception must exclude this situation through legal provisions. The meaning of pluralism is to recognize the diversity in thought and opinion, the freedom to exchange and debate between different political and social organizations equal before the law and equal in free elections to gain the confidence of society. A political party can still seize the right to govern society within the limits of the law, but only with the confidence of society through democratic mechanisms and not through a pre-determined imposition.

The nation's prosperity demands the equal right of each member of society to contribute his/her energy and intellect, to no one's exclusion. To genuinely realize such a democratic political regime will create conditions for national unity and concord in order to jointly rebuild the nation. The unity and unification of the nation must be on the basis of the struggle to build a common Motherland of free and equal citizens, and not on the basis of a compulsory "unanimity" with the ideology of a dictatorship, especially when such ideology is retrograde and remote from the demands of the nation and of the times.

A political regime with democracy, national unity and concord is an urgent demand, of a life-or-death nature, for our nation today.

II.4  The State

An economic regime and a political regime with the above-mentioned contents must be formalized in the Constitution, particularly in the provisions on State organization. That is a democratic State under the rule of law. In the conditions of our country, the most appropriate framework of such a legislative State is that of a parliamentary regime (ie, the National Assembly). The National Assembly is the supreme organ of power (a reality, not just a formality) that also directly exercises legislative powers. The National Assembly elects a Government (executive) and a Supreme Court (judicial) - two mutually independent organs that are only accountable to the National Assembly. The crucial point here is that the National Assembly must be elected in genuinely free and fair elections, and that no political party or clan is pre-determined to have the absolute right to stand above and govern the State. Any political party with a majority in the National Assembly can implement its line and policies, but must follow legal regulations. A State organized on such basis belongs to the entire people and, truly, also to the working people because the overwhelming majority are working people. The common task of the State is to put forward legal provisions, policies and decisions to create a favorable environment and secure conditions for developing the economic and political regime as presented above. With a market economy and a political democracy, the State will have the opportunity to vigorously pursue a foreign policy of peace and cooperation, creating a favorable international climate for rapid national development. An important strategic task of the State is to bolster and boost the nation's labor capacity and intellectual capability, the educational and training system, to develop social equality through the appropriate tax policies for income redistribution and through social insurance, social welfare, universal education, … - these tasks being gradually fulfilled along with the nation's economic development.

III. Urgent Solutions

III.1  Demand for and objective of one solution

In today's state of total crisis, it is not yet time to consider long-term strategies whose objectives are without specific contents, whose socio-economic targets are attractive but without any realistic basis during the next ten, fifteen years. The most important matter before us is the necessity for resolutely overcoming the crisis, for edifying a minimally sound situation to permit normal activities in the market economy and democratic political regime that we wish to build, with the freedom to develop each capability in society. It is necessary to endeavor and to resolutely implement necessary solutions so that, within the next three or five years, the crisis will be fundamentally overcome, the situation can be considered as normal, the market economy will function normally, and the State will have a practical basis for contemplating the next steps.

The characteristics of the present crisis are: high inflation (at times approaching 500% before 1989, and hovering around 100% in 1990); a sharp reduction in real wages (about one-third of 1985 figures, and about half of 1989 figures) which are no longer meaningful for sustaining a livelihood; a reduction in purchasing power; a large budget deficit (new currency to compensate for the deficit amounts to about one-quarter of the budget in 1990, and probably a larger proportion in 1991); a State apparatus cumbersome yet replete with deficiencies and corruption; a chaotic society with irrational injustices, rampant social ills, and deteriorating education and health care.

Not too many urgent objectives should be put forward for the upcoming period. However, to really overcome the crisis and to drive the nation into a sphere of normal development, there must at least be the following objectives:

  • Slow inflation to an annual rate of less than 10%, principally through cutting the budget deficit, reducing the proportion of new currency to less than 10% of the State budget.
  • Thoroughly reform the State apparatus. Cut by at least 50% the amount of wages from the State budget in 1991-1992. Guarantee minimum wages for government workers (minimum compensation of 80kg/month of rice, ie, average of 120kg/month). Abolish all subsidies, special privileges and powers.
  • Improve investment opportunities to rescue education, health, scientific research, … from collapse. Mobilize capital inside and outside the country to bolster and modernize some minimal infrastructures to serve economic development.

Below are proposals for several urgent solutions (there are certainly many other possibilities not yet considered) to achieve the above minimal objectives in the few years ahead.

III.2  Socio-economic solutions

There must be resolute, at times severe, measures to realize the above minimal objectives. Normal conditions for development are out of the question if the very modest objectives above cannot be achieved. These solutions may include:

  • Resolutely switch to a market economy. Perfect a legal system compatible with the demands of a market economy, especially the recognition and protection of the right to private ownership. Really create conditions for a market economy to develop, guarantee the confidence in the freedom to foster all entrepreneurial capacities, abolish all restrictive and discriminatory regimes toward the non-State economic sectors.
  • The cornerstone in this period is to carry out privatization. Privatization is to transform into a market economy, and is also the essential method to provide the State budget with a sufficient source of income for expenditures toward paring down government staff, increasing wages, investing in the infrastructure, education, health, … without resorting to new currency for deficit compensation. Privatization is through the sale of current State assets, above all in the spheres which the private sector can effectively sustain (services, trade, light industry, housing, real estate, …). The State will form a Special Committee on Privatization to guarantee a consistent implementation and adequate income toward the State budget.
  • Reorganize the State apparatus to fulfill the role of State management in a market economy. Reduce government staff, and provide compensation to jobless former government workers so that they can adjust their careers and livelihood. Raise the salary of remaining government workers to recover discipline, ethics and job effectiveness. Implement a regime of competitive selection and labor contract throughout the State system. Resolutely abolish all subsidies, ie, housing, automobiles, living expenses; abolish special privileges and powers. The State will not fund the budget of entities outside the State apparatus (such as the Party organs, associations, …). A Special Committee to Combat Corruption, Special Privileges and Powers will be appointed by the National Assembly, to which it is accountable, and not appointed by the Government nor within the responsibility of the heads of governmental organs.
  • To increase the effectiveness of State economic management means, first of all, to specially strengthen and perfect the financial and banking system. Perfect the legal system and various tax regulations, in order to create a lasting confidence for production and entrepreneurial activities, to encourage capital investment from inside and outside the country toward economic development; at the same time, to formulate effective organizational measures for adequate tax collection into the budget, and for severe punishment of tax evasion and corruption during tax collection. On the basis of inflation under control, stabilize banking activities. Maintain a regime of savings interest rates higher than the inflation rate, and struggle to reduce interest rates along with the course of inflation (when inflation is less than 10%/year, interest rates can be pegged at less than 2%/month). Rapidly evolve the banking organization system to be consistent with a market economy, to create a stable and confident monetary market for the promotion of capital and investment.

III.3  Political reform

Reforming the political system is of decisive significance for the entire cause of renovation. Our objective is to achieve a political system with democracy, national unity and concord, as presented in previous sections. We hope and believe that there is a stable, peaceful and conciliatory path toward such an objective. At present, the Vietnamese Communist Party governs the country - totally and absolutely. For this reason, the best and only course for our nation is for the Party - in the supreme interest of the nation and with its responsibility before history - to renovate itself and to initiate a path for the entire nation to fully achieve the cause of renovation. Under these conditions, in conjunction with the implementation of the socio-economic solutions, the following reform measures may be realized in 1991-1992:

  • The Viet Nam Fatherland Front, with the support of the Vietnamese Communist Party, and in political consultation with all forces in the nation, personalities and intellectuals inside and outside the country, to organize a National Convention with the common theme and goal of National Unity and Reconstruction. This would be the first step in the wide mobilization of every intellectual potential of the nation toward the cause of national reconstruction and development in a true spirit of national unity, concord and reconciliation among free and equal Vietnamese men and women.
  • Reorganize the current State apparatus, especially by establishing a Reform Government for the critical 1991-1992 period, which must be a strong government composed of individuals of true capability and intellect to effectively implement urgent socio-economic solutions in the stage of overcoming the crisis, and creating a sound environment for national development in the latter stage.
  • Fully realize several democratic freedoms, especially freedom of thought, freedom of expression and of the press; freedom of meeting and association (within legally-specified limits to prevent the emergence of organizations bent on vengeance and violence). This action will create a healthy democratic atmosphere for developing every intellectual capacity of the nation to serve the cause of national reconstruction and development.
  • Elect a National Assembly in genuinely free and fair elections in 1992 or early 1993. The National Assembly will conceive and ratify a Constitution specifying a market economy with State macro-regulation and a political regime with democracy, national unity and concord.

III.4  Preparing a post-crisis development strategy

In the process of realizing the urgent solutions to overcome the crisis, a strategy of national development will be prepared for the subsequent stage. Such a strategy, in the condition of a market economy, will be different from the so-called strategies formulated in the previous central command economy.

The transition to a market economy is a necessary, but not sufficient, condition for resolving every problem. Certainly many new, more complex and diverse problems will emerge and demand fresh solutions. In the initial stage, there must be active policies to support the development of commodity production, when such production is not yet varied, when the market is still embryonic and has not yet attained a high level of development; afterwards, there certainly will appear many problems requiring State intervention of varying degrees. The strategy for such a developed stage will no longer be like a State centralized plan as before, but will embody contents of an analytical, forecasting, and predictive nature, specifying measures for State macro-regulation.

IV. The Vietnamese Communist Party and the Reform Process

Resolutely overcoming the crisis to achieve a market economy and a democratic political regime is a demand and matter of survival for the nation. How and what results this reform process of such a decisive nature will achieve will depend principally on the Vietnamese Communist Party, the current party in power.

With ideals of patriotism, the Party has assembled within its ranks a large number of outstanding members of the nation, and has led and mobilized the entire nation to victory in the liberation struggle, to the country's independence. Nevertheless, we must recognize that theories of communism and "socialism" - with their exaggeration of class contradictions and struggle, with their hurried imposition of an economy with public ownership, centralized management, a regime with the Party's monopoly on power, … - have brought our country many privations: a nation divided and paralyzed, a devastated economy, an impoverished and backward livelihood, isolation from the civilized world, …

The choice of the communist ideal has its own historical reasons and had an active effect in the national liberation struggle. But theories of communism and socialism, in their original conception as well as in the practical models that we have followed as the basis for national reconstruction, are real errors, and have proven to be inappropriate for the practical demands to liberate our nation from feudalism, backwardness and poverty, into prosperity, freedom and happiness.

Based on the supreme interest of the nation, from the real and urgent demands of our country's livelihood, we must be lucid and brave to liberate ourselves from all manifestations of the lust for power and all enslavement to irrelevant beliefs, to regain freedom of reason and cognition, to imbue and master all sources of intellect from humanity, in order to jointly discover the most appropriate route for our nation today. The Party was grand in uniting and leading the country in national liberation, and will now be grander if, after achieving liberation, the Party transfers power to the entire nation in which it is the vanguard, instead of retaining for the Party all power, which is essentially wielded by a minority within the Party.

The most attractive course for our nation today is for the Party to renovate itself and to lead the initiative, together with the entire nation, in realizing thorough reforms, overcoming the crisis, building the country along the lines of a market economy and a truly democratic political regime. The Party will no longer stand at the head of a tired, exhausted and isolated nation, but will place itself within the nation, and will always struggle to retain the trust as vanguard of the nation - a nation that is free, full of energy and intellect, integrated into civilized humanity.

The Party has been recognized by the nation as the vanguard in the patriotic cause. Patriotism is the essence, and every theory or ideology must be chosen and filtered to serve the patriotic goal. Marxism-Leninism and the socialist model that we have followed clearly contain many elements which are inappropriate to the fulfillment of that goal for our nation, as well as have proven irrelevant for the development needs of the modern world. Therefore, the desire is that, with the great potential within the Party and the support of the entire nation, the Party will find adequate strength to renovate itself - to renovate its ideology and theoretical basis, to renovate its organization, to renovate in determining its role within the nation - in order to retain the confidence as the nation's vanguard in this stage, to bring the nation along the path for the sound development of a prosperous market economy and a society with democracy, national unity and concord.
